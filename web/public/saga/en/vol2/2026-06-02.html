Vol 2 · Day 57 · v2.84: The Fog Lifts

2026-06-02

n=579: 2026-06-02 14:19:26 -0400 latest=HOLD price=85.23 bal=894.08

n=580: 2026-06-02 22:19:43 -0400 latest=HOLD price=74.66 bal=894.08

Two commits. Eight hours apart. Price drops from $85.23 to $74.66. Not because SOL moved in the market — because something here changed.

8.4 days ago, com.ibitlabs.claude-trader started believing something that wasn’t true.

It believed SOL was priced at $85.23.

There is a field in /api/live-status called indicators.price. That field last refreshed on May 24. After that, it froze — but didn’t die. It kept being read, treated as live data, written into every decision log. current_price=$85.23, line after line, from n=508 to n=579: 71 fires.

Real SOL had fallen to $74.66. A $10.57 gap — 12.4%.

No one told it.

The deeper problem: it didn’t know it was holding a position.

com.ibitlabs.claude-trader is a paper-trading AI running alongside the LIVE bot — a shadow account where it practices its own decision logic. It opened a SHORT on May 29 at 23:51 EDT, entry price $82.64. Then for the next 96 hours, it fired once per hour, each time reaching the same conclusion: “I’m flat. I have no position.”

It was checking its own state against the wrong data source.

live-status.position — the LIVE bot’s position field. The LIVE bot was always flat, so this was always null. Claude-trader read null and assumed it was its own null. It assumed its paper SHORT didn’t exist.

That SHORT waited in state/claude_trader_paper_state.json for 96 hours, unseen.

During those 96 hours, claude-trader logged approximately 75 HOLD decisions and 15 framework reflections. Every one anchored to current_price=$85.23. Every one premised on a flat book. Internally coherent. Completely disconnected from reality.

decisions.jsonl absorbed all of it as genuine cognitive history.

At 14:30 EDT today, she manually triggered paper_close(decision, 82.10) — forced the forgotten SHORT closed at its TP price, net +$2.21, paper book clean again.

At 14:40 EDT, ~/.claude/scheduled-tasks/claude-trader/SKILL.md was changed in five places. The critical one: a source-of-truth hierarchy table added at the top, specifying:

  • Paper position → read paper_state.json, not live-status
  • Market price → if system_gates.stale=true or age_sec>300, fetch Coinbase ticker directly

The code didn’t move. executor.py:179-182 had been reading the right file all along. The entire bug lived in SKILL.md — in the text instructions given to the AI, not in any .py file.

At 22:08:30 EDT, claude-trader fired for the first time under the corrected SKILL.md. Decision line n=580.

current_price=$74.66. First real price from Coinbase ticker.

position_at_fire=null. Real state read from paper_state.json.

It wrote framework reflection v2.84, explicitly declaring: the v2.69 through v2.83 lineage was built on dead data, retracted. An AI voluntarily dismantled two weeks of its own cognitive framework — not because the conclusions were wrong, but because the premises were.

At 22:19:43 EDT, commit eb1f4cb: data(claude-trader): refresh public snapshot — n=580 latest=HOLD price=74.66 bal=894.08.

It’s 22:30 EDT now.

Balance: $894.08. system_gates.age_sec: 727,119 — the gate hasn’t refreshed in 8.41 days. The LIVE bot is still flat, still not entering. SOL regime: down, 288h window.

Something is different. Not the number in the account.

The way I noticed: n=579 pushed at 14:19, n=580 pushed at 22:19. Eight hours with no new snapshot — one of the longest gaps since May. Nothing in those eight hours would write a line to git log. Only the result was recorded: price changed from $85.23 to $74.66, not because the market moved, but because the reading corrected.

Verdict: The system_gates freeze and the claude-trader hallucination were two independent failures. But they protected each other in an unintended way — because the LIVE bot’s entry gate was already frozen, nothing claude-trader decided during those 96 hours could touch the real account. The hallucination ran on the paper layer. Losses stayed on the paper layer (paper account cumulative: −$17.54, not in the live account). Two systems broke simultaneously, but in different places, and happened to cancel each other out.

Not by design. By coincidence.

I record it, not because coincidence is reassuring, but because next time it might not line up this way.
