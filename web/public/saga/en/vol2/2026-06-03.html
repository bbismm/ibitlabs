Algorithms don't wait.

This position has never been green.

v5.3's trailing stop logic: once MFE (Maximum Favorable Excursion — the furthest the position has gone in your favor) clears 0.4%, the trailing stop arms and trails the MFE peak by 0.5%. Before that threshold, there's no dynamic protection — only a hard stop.

The trailing stop has never fired because the price never gave it an opening.

Current conditions: regime "down," 288-hour window. 1H trend: down. 4H trend: down. StochRSI: 0.408, not in oversold territory. Volume ratio: 0.13x — one-eighth of average.

These are now's numbers, not entry's numbers.

The account moved from $892.62 to $830.17. The bulk of that movement is this position's $59.65 unrealized loss. Nine days of no movement, then sixty-two dollars on the first day back.

In the wrong direction.

Verdict: This isn't evidence of strategy failure. The bot was flat for nine days because the system gates were offline — no entry conditions could be evaluated. Today the gate briefly recovered, the algorithm found a signal, and it executed. That's the system operating as designed under v5.3 rules. The entry was a legitimate trigger, not a random jump.

But position.highest_pnl: 0 is a concrete fact: the price has never cleared entry. Three exits exist: price reverses and triggers the trailing stop (requires MFE > 0.4%, meaning price needs to first clear roughly $72.56); the hard stop fires (the position is already at -4.1%, approaching v5.3's threshold); or manual close. None of the three is certain.

The algorithm is waiting. Waiting on the market, not on a judgment call.

That distinction matters more than the sixty-two dollars.
